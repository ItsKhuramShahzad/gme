Estimation of Genuine Multipartite Entanglement in Half-Filled Hubbard Rings

Khuram Shahzad and Guido Goldoni
University of Modena and Reggio Emilia (UNIMORE),
Department of Physics, Informatics and Mathematics

Genuine multipartite entanglement (GME) is a central resource for quantum
technologies and a sensitive probe of many-body correlations. In
itinerant-electron systems it is hard to quantify, because charge
fluctuations, subsystem reduction, and temperature produce mixed spin states.
Here we investigate spin GME in finite, half-filled Hubbard rings.

We solve the Hubbard model -- nearest-neighbour hopping t, on-site Coulomb
repulsion U -- by exact diagonalization for rings of N = 2-8 sites at half
filling (N electrons, periodic boundary conditions), in both the Sz = 0 sector
and the full fixed-particle-number Hilbert space.

From a correlated eigenstate |Psi> we choose a cluster of n sites (n <= N) and
trace out the remaining N-n sites to obtain their reduced density matrix
rho_n. Projecting each selected site onto single occupation (projector P) and
renormalizing gives the n-spin reduced density matrix
Gamma^(n) = P rho_n P / Tr(P rho_n P), a 2^n x 2^n matrix on the local spin
configurations {up,down}^n. This isolates the spin degrees of freedom from the
charge while preserving the fermionic symmetries; Gamma^(n) is Hermitian,
positive semidefinite, and unit-trace. Finite-temperature states follow as
Gibbs mixtures of the eigenstate matrices.

For a pure state, GME is quantified by the genuinely multipartite concurrence:
the weakest bipartite entanglement over all bipartitions A|B of the cluster,
C_GME = min_{A|B} sqrt( 2 [ 1 - Tr(rho_A^2) ] ), where rho_A is the reduced
state of part A. The extracted and thermal states are generally mixed, so
their GME is defined through the convex-roof extension,
C_GME(rho) = min sum_i p_i C_GME(phi_i) over all pure-state decompositions
rho = sum_i p_i |phi_i><phi_i|. This non-convex optimization uses a
generalized conjugate-gradient method on the unitary group, cross-checked by a
Stiefel-manifold parametrization.

We map C_GME as a function of cluster size n, coupling ratio t/U, temperature
T, and ring size N. Ground-state GME grows with cluster size, approaching its
maximum for the whole ring; temperature suppresses it on a scale set by the
spectral gap, so entanglement survives to higher T for larger t/U; near
separability, thermal population of entangled excited states can instead
activate GME. For n = 2 the measure reduces exactly to the Wootters
concurrence, an analytic benchmark.

This framework links correlated electronic structure to effective spin
entanglement, clarifying how genuine multipartite correlations respond to
interactions, subsystem reduction, and temperature; the same construction
extends to molecular pi systems such as benzene.
